Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Traffic Signal Control

Q-Learning (Off-Policy TD)
Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α [ r + γ · maxₐ' Q(s', a') - Q(s, a) ]

Q-Learning is an off-policy Temporal Difference algorithm that directly estimates the optimal action-value function regardless of what action the exploration policy chooses.

  • Behavior: Bold, pursues maximum estimated downstream reward.
  • In Traffic: Aggressively prioritizes lanes with maximum waiting backlog, switching signals decisively.
SARSA (On-Policy TD)
Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α [ r + γ · Q(s', a') - Q(s, a) ]

SARSA (State-Action-Reward-State-Action) is an on-policy algorithm. It updates its value estimate using the actual action $a'$ chosen by its current $\epsilon$-greedy policy.

  • Behavior: Cautious, factors in potential random mistakes caused by exploration.
  • In Traffic: Prevents rapid oscillations or premature phase toggling when yellow clearance delays cause penalties.
Model-Based RL (Dyna-Q)
T(s, a) → s', R(s, a) → r + N Planning Steps

Model-Based RL builds an internal predictive world model of how vehicle queues respond to signal changes. In addition to real-world experience, it performs hallucinated planning updates.

  • Behavior: Highly sample-efficient; learns much faster with fewer real-world traffic jams.
  • In Traffic: Anticipates queue buildups before they become gridlocks, maintaining maximum vehicle throughput.
